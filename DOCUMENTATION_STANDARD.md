# Documentation and evidence standard

## Scope and purpose

All project documentation shall present the current economic transition framework, its requirements, supporting analysis, limitations, and research needs in a formal scholarly style appropriate to doctoral research. This is an independent research proposal; dissertation-level presentation does not imply academic accreditation, peer review, or demonstrated feasibility.

## Current-state presentation

Documents shall stand alone and describe the current proposal coherently. They shall exclude conversation summaries, quotations from internal exchanges, drafting narratives, accounts of superseded proposals, and explanations of why one version replaced another. Analytical justification for a current provision is appropriate. Historical accounts of its adoption are not part of the substantive documents. Git history and platform proposal and ballot records provide revision traceability without reproducing those narratives in current documentation.

Preserve the complete relationship among economic security, individual agency, diverse ambitions, productive ownership, contestable institutional power, and a survivable transition. Use precise definitions, consistent terminology, explicit scope, research questions, methods, alternatives, limitations, and requirements for improvement. Unresolved provisions shall be marked Requires definition, Requires evidence, or Requires validation, with the work needed to resolve them.

## Evidence and attribution

Every externally verifiable factual assertion, statistic, quotation, attributed idea, and description of an existing program shall carry a nearby numbered citation. Stable notation such as [R01] is permitted. Every cited source shall have a matching entry in a References section at the end of that document. A link to a separate register alone is insufficient for a standalone document.

Each entry shall identify the author or responsible institution, full title, publication date when verified, publisher where relevant, URL or DOI, relevant section or page where available, and access or verification date. Missing metadata shall be labeled unverified rather than invented. Distinguish sources actually examined from candidate literature awaiting appraisal.

Sources must support the exact associated claim within its stated jurisdiction, period, population, and methodological limits. Prefer primary research, official statistics, original legislation, and program documentation. Verify quotations and passages, identify secondary reporting, and disclose material counterevidence. A citation to a related topic does not establish support for a claim.

Clearly distinguish sourced findings, project proposals, normative judgments, analytical inferences, modeling assumptions, and unresolved hypotheses. Normative proposals do not require fictitious external authority, but their factual premises require evidence. Unsupported claims shall be flagged explicitly and shall not be reported as established findings. Voting determines project positions, not empirical truth.

## Reproducibility and transparency

Models shall disclose data provenance, units, time periods, assumptions, calculations, sensitivity ranges, and limitations. Material conflicts of interest and evidence gaps shall be disclosed. Publication must not imply verification, legal validity, operational capability, or successful outcomes that have not been established.

The evidence standard reflects the project's requirement for transparent public institutions. Review shall evaluate whether a reader can trace each factual conclusion to its evidence and distinguish evidence from policy preference.

## Publication requirements

Before publication, check claim-to-source correspondence, reference completeness, definitions, unresolved issues, and consistency across the overview, complete plan, requirements register, and generated editions. Regenerate PDF and text editions from the current source and inspect all PDF pages. Apply the same standard to future contributions and templates.
