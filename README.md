# Designing a Fair Economy

**Economic Transition Framework — working draft v0.1**  
Project owner: Steve Smith  
Source plan: September 24, 2026

## Purpose

Explore how economic systems can improve basic security and access to productive ownership while preserving human liberty, personal choice, entrepreneurship, ambition, competitive markets, and peaceful institutional correction.

This is a research and framework-development project. Its proposals are provisional; affordability, effectiveness, enforceability, and public acceptance have not been established.

## Working direction

- Provide sufficient economic security for meaningful choice while preserving diverse ambitions and outcomes.
- Explore broader participation in productive ownership and future growth.
- Keep economic and institutional power open to challenge and correction.
- Test alternatives alongside functioning institutions, reuse existing capacity, and expand only on evidence.
- Protect vulnerable households from disproportionate transition costs.
- Identify real labor, resources, financing, and administrative capacity rather than assuming a transition is costless.

## Education for service

The project owner's proposal is that accepting educational support creates at least a one-year service obligation regardless of graduation, with capability-based service matching for participants with disabilities. These are design inputs, not settled contractual provisions.

Paid service, proportional additional years, vocational eligibility, waivers, and specific governance mechanisms remain assistant proposals awaiting explicit owner decisions. Participation in education does not by itself establish sustained employment capacity. Actual capability, accommodations, changing circumstances, placement failure, and practical exit options require examination.

These are planned phases, not completed milestones. A United States civilian case study is a proposed starting boundary, not an adopted geographic restriction.

## Research standards

Distinguish owner proposals, working synthesis, assistant suggestions, and empirical or legal claims. Continued discussion does not imply approval. Every recommendation must explain its rationale, who pays, who bears risk, what could falsify it, and how it could be revised or stopped.

Evaluate fiscal costs, real resource requirements, and distributional effects separately. Compare proposals with the existing system and plausible alternatives. Do not treat useful public service as cash revenue automatically available to fund tuition.
