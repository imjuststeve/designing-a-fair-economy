Economic Transition Framework Project Plan
Economic security, liberty, opportunity, and distributed power
Working draft v0.1 | September 24, 2026 | Project owner: Steve Smith
Purpose and intended result
Develop and critically evaluate a framework for improving economic security and access to
productive ownership while preserving personal freedom, markets, ambition, and peaceful
institutional correction. The immediate result is a documented, evidence-based proposal with
explicit uncertainties. Adoption, legislation, and a live pilot require later decisions.
Use the supplied conversation and working overview as the starting record. The previous
three-page PDF was an abridgment and omitted the references; it should not replace the fuller
discussion as the provenance record. This plan restores a traceable reference register and
separates user proposals from assistant suggestions.
Scope
• Define economic security, meaningful choice, concentrated power, contestability, and capture in
measurable terms.
• Compare financing, ownership, competition, governance, and transition options. Develop the
education-for-service compact as a specific candidate mechanism.
• Test affordability, labor capacity, distributional effects, accessibility, coercion risks, and
institutional failure. Produce a pilot design only if the evidence supports one.
Working constraints from the overview
Protect basic security during change; preserve diverse ambitions and outcomes; avoid deliberately
dismantling essential capacity before alternatives work; reuse institutions where feasible; make
intermediate stages useful; distribute authority; and allow future generations to revise the
framework. These constraints form a provisional baseline, not a claim that their implementation is
already solved.
Definition of completion for this research cycle
A versioned framework with a glossary, verified evidence register, compared policy options,
reproducible cost model, service-compact specification, failure scenarios, and unresolved-decision
log. Every recommendation must state its rationale, who pays, who bears risk, what could falsify
it, and how it could be revised or stopped.
Recommended starting boundary: use one United States civilian case study for tractable
modeling, then test transferability to other jurisdictions. This is a proposed research choice, not an
agreed geographic limitation. No claim of originality or proven superiority is assumed.

Preserve the status of each idea
Status
Meaning and initial examples
User proposal
Education support creates at least one year of service regardless of graduation. Disability
should prompt capability-based service matching. These are design inputs, not settled
legal provisions.
Working synthesis
Economic floor, wider ownership, contestable power, incremental transition, and
protection of vulnerable households summarize the prior overview and remain open to
refinement.
Assistant proposal
Paid service, proportional additional years, vocational eligibility, waivers, and specific
governance mechanisms were suggested in the discussion. Require explicit project-owner

Empirical or legal
claim
Requires a source, passage, date, jurisdiction, and limitations. A citation does not
establish that the complete framework works.
Roles
Project owner: Steve sets priorities and accepts or rejects proposed principles. Research and
drafting support: collect evidence, compare alternatives, document assumptions, and maintain
versions. Independent reviewers: economics, public finance, labor, disability access, public
administration, and law; recruitment and availability are not assumed.
Working records
• Decision log: ID, question, options, evidence IDs, decision, rationale, date, owner, and review
trigger.
• Claim register: exact claim, originating message or document, source and passage, supporting
or conflicting evidence, confidence, and scope limits.
• Risk register: affected people, mechanism of harm, leading indicator, mitigation, residual risk,
and stop condition.
• Change log: retain prior wording and explain substantive changes. Silence or continued
discussion does not count as approval.
Review rhythm
Work in focused research sessions. End each with completed outputs, changed assumptions,
unresolved questions, and the next decision. Conduct a phase review before advancing; record
proceed, revise, defer, or reject. Publication and outreach remain separate actions requiring
explicit authorization.

Research workstreams
A Economic security and household resilience
Specify the necessities included in a floor, eligibility, delivery routes, and geographic cost
differences. Compare cash, services, and combinations. Model benefit cliffs, access failures,
administrative burden, and take-up. Deliver a floor options paper and household profiles. Starting
references: R06, R08, R11.
B Ownership and competitive markets
Compare employee ownership, diversified citizen funds, cooperatives, retirement participation,
and profit sharing. Examine investment risk, voting rights, liquidity, portability, inheritance, and
the danger of concentrating both wages and savings in one employer. Address natural monopolies
and market entry. Deliver an ownership and competition options matrix. The existing references
are insufficient for this workstream; add primary research and outcome evaluations.
C Governance and resistance to capture
Map who appoints, funds, audits, disciplines, and removes each decision-maker. Test donor
influence, revolving doors, procurement favoritism, collusion between oversight bodies, and
capture of citizen funds. Compare distributed authority with coordination costs. Deliver an
authority map and threat register. Starting references: R01-R03, R19.
D Financing and real capacity
Identify start-up and recurring costs, who ultimately bears each funding mechanism, training
bottlenecks, and capacity that can be reused. Compare taxation, spending reallocation, limited
borrowing, and ownership returns. Deliver a cost model and resource inventory. R04, R06-R08
provide context; their settings and assumptions must be preserved.
E Education and public service
Specify the compact, compare existing programs, and test whether it adds useful capacity. Include
accessible placements, completion failure, and government failure to place participants. Deliver a
policy options paper and draft participant terms. Starting references: R12-R18, R20. Military
programs are contextual comparisons, not automatic templates for civilian enforcement.
F Transition and public legitimacy
Compare sequencing, compensation, participation, and reversibility. Ask affected people about
concrete choices and costs. Record how consultation changes proposals. Deliver an
implementation sequence, evaluation design, and communication brief that discloses trade-offs.
Starting references: R01, R03-R05, R09-R11.

Education for service design agenda
Retain the user proposal of a minimum one-year obligation after accepting educational support,
independent of graduation. Test its operation before adopting contract language. Education
participation can coexist with highly variable work capacity; placement must assess actual abilities
and changing circumstances.
Decision
Options and questions to resolve
Acceptance and
accrual
Does obligation begin at signing, disbursement, or enrollment? Is there a cooling-off
period? How is a small or reversed payment treated? Compare fixed minimum,
funded-year accrual, and a capped obligation.
Eligibility and benefit
Admissions standards, residency, prior education, program quality, tuition caps, living
support, part-time study, and vocational pathways. Prevent provider price inflation and
low-value enrollment.
Pay and work
conditions
Assess normal salary and benefits, wage parity, hours, caregiving, relocation, part-time
equivalence, and representation. Paid service is recommended but requires a recorded
decision.
Placement
Define qualifying public benefit, approved employers, participant choice, local or remote
work, supervision, accreditation, and a maximum placement wait. Decide what happens
if government cannot provide suitable work.
Disability and hardship
Accommodation, changing capacity, episodic illness, caring duties, deferment, appeal,
and genuine incapacity. No automatic assumption that studying establishes sustained
employment capacity. Waiver criteria remain open.
Withdrawal and breach
Examine transfer, withdrawal, proportionate repayment, hardship relief, and
government breach. Test whether the exit terms preserve practical freedom. Legal
review must assess enforceability and rights.
Additional public value
Verify useful work, training and supervision costs, retention, job displacement, wage
effects, and whether participants would have served anyway.
Equity
Compare participation and burden by income, disability, location, age, and family status.
Examine whether wealthy participants can buy freedom from terms borne by others.
Required output: a plain-language specimen agreement plus a decision matrix, with unresolved
clauses visibly marked. The agreement remains a research draft until independent legal and
accessibility review and owner approval.

Feasibility model and evaluation
Keep three accounts distinct
• Fiscal account: annual public cash outlays, receipts, debt service, and contingent liabilities.
• Resource account: workers, training seats, supervision, facilities, materials, and time diverted
from other activities.
• Distribution account: household costs and benefits by income, disability, region, and
participation status.
Minimum costing specification
For each cohort and year, estimate education funding + participant pay and benefits +
administration + accommodation + supervision + facilities and financing costs. Subtract only
separately justified budget savings or receipts. Present gross costs even when net costs are
reported. Public service output is a benefit to assess, not cash revenue that can automatically fund
tuition.
Compare the compact with ordinary hiring, scholarships without service, apprenticeships, and
targeted retention incentives. Vary enrollment, completion, placement delay, wage growth,
service length, supervisory load, dropout, and long-term retention. Track discount rates and
inflation assumptions; report ranges rather than a single confident forecast.
Evaluation measures
Outcome
Measurement approach
Economic security
Disposable resources after essential costs; hardship, arrears, access delays, and
benefit cliffs.
Freedom and fairness
Real exit options; unwanted placement; complaints; differences in burden and access
across groups.
Public capacity
Additional staffed hours or validated services relative to the baseline, adjusted for
displacement and supervision.
Fiscal performance
Gross and net spending, cost per additional service unit, liabilities, and cost overruns.
Governance
Concentration of authority, conflicts, audit coverage, complaint resolution, and
reversibility.
Set thresholds before a pilot, with affected participants and reviewers. Use credible comparison
groups where feasible and disclose selection bias, spillovers, and uncertainty. A small successful
pilot does not establish national scalability. R01 and R09-R10 inform evaluation and engagement;
no numerical targets are adopted here.
